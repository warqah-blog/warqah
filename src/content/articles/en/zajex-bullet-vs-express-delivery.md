Not every ecommerce order is equally urgent. A customer buying a gift hours before an occasion needs a different service from one who accepts delivery later in the day. [ZAJEX](https://zajex.co/en) addresses both cases in Riyadh with **Bullet delivery in 1–4 hours** and **Express delivery within 24 hours**.

<div class="answer-box">
<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Choose ZAJEX Bullet for urgent orders where arrival speed affects the purchase, provided the address is inside an eligible coverage zone. Choose Express for scheduled daily orders where delivery within 24 hours meets the promise. The strongest operating model is not forcing one service onto every order, but selecting by the customer promise, address, parcel readiness and full cost.</p>

## What is ZAJEX Bullet?

Bullet is ZAJEX's fastest Riyadh service. According to the [official website](https://zajex.co/en), a driver collects an eligible ready order and delivers it within **one to four hours**.

It suits orders that lose value when delayed, including:

- Gifts and purchases tied to an occasion later that day.
- Products the customer needs urgently.
- High-value orders where the store wants a premium experience.
- Campaigns that use delivery within hours as a checkout advantage.

The delivery window still depends on the merchant having the parcel ready. A package waiting two hours for internal approval or packing does not become fast simply because the carrier is fast.

## What is ZAJEX Express?

Express is a same-day or **within-24-hour** service. ZAJEX describes scheduled collection from the merchant's warehouse followed by delivery throughout the day.

Express can be the better fit when:

- The order belongs to a daily shipment batch.
- The customer does not need arrival within a few hours.
- The merchant wants same-day speed with more room to schedule warehouse collection.
- Checkout promises “within 24 hours” rather than “within 1–4 hours.”

Express is not slow delivery. It is faster than conventional multi-day shipping while allowing orders and routes to be organised more efficiently.

Do not advertise delivery within hours to every customer. Bullet should only appear when the address, order time and fulfilment operation can support the promise.

## A simple decision rule for every order

Ask four questions before creating the shipment:

1. **What did we promise the customer?** If it is within hours, test Bullet eligibility. If it is within the day, Express is often enough.
2. **Is the parcel ready now?** An unprepared order does not benefit from urgent delivery.
3. **Is the address eligible?** ZAJEX states that Bullet does not automatically cover every order.
4. **Is speed worth the cost difference?** Use current rates in the merchant account because ZAJEX does not publish a universal public price list.

A concise operating rule is:

> Ready parcel + eligible address + promise within four hours = Bullet. Otherwise, when delivery within 24 hours is acceptable = Express.

## Do not choose from the shipment price alone

A correct comparison includes the business effect of the delivery promise. Consider:

- Base shipment price.
- COD or return fees, where applicable.
- Customer-support cost caused by a late order.
- Cancellation or refusal risk caused by waiting.
- The value of charging for an urgent upgrade.

Bullet can justify a higher cost when it protects a valuable sale or fulfils a paid urgent promise. Using it for every order without a real customer need can increase cost without creating visible value.

## How to present both options at checkout

Use customer-facing names instead of internal service names alone:

- **Urgent delivery in 1–4 hours** — only for eligible addresses and order times.
- **Delivery within 24 hours** — the standard local option.

Explain when the clock starts: at order confirmation or once the parcel is ready? Set cut-off times for late orders. A measurable promise is more useful than the word “fast.”

## How integration and tracking support the choice

ZAJEX says store integration sends orders to its operations centre and prepares labels with fewer manual steps. It also publishes a [merchant API](https://developers.zajex.co/) for creating, retrieving and tracking delivery orders.

Tracking is especially important for Bullet because its window is short and delays become visible quickly. Express also benefits when the store is moving a large daily batch.

Useful timestamps include:

- Order creation and fulfilment completion.
- Courier collection.
- Out-for-delivery status.
- Delivery or failed attempt.
- Failure reason and next action.

## When to use both services together

A hybrid model is often more balanced than one service for everything.

### Paid urgent upgrade

Make Express the default and offer Bullet as a paid upgrade whenever eligible.

### Order-value rule

Use Bullet for high-value or high-priority customers and Express for routine orders.

### Order-time rule

Offer Bullet before a clear cut-off, then route later orders to Express according to the displayed promise.

### Product rule

Assign occasion-based and time-sensitive products to Bullet, leaving non-urgent categories on Express.

## Metrics that show whether the decision works

Measure each service separately:

- Percentage delivered within the promised window.
- Average internal fulfilment time before collection.
- First-attempt delivery success rate.
- “Where is my order?” contacts per 100 shipments.
- Cancellation or refusal rate.
- Full cost per successful delivery.
- Post-delivery customer rating.

If Bullet misses its promise because packing is slow, fix the warehouse before increasing urgent volume. If Express gives the same satisfaction at lower cost for a product category, make it that category's default.

## Common selection mistakes

### Offering Bullet to every address

It is limited to eligible Riyadh zones. Check coverage before confirming the promise.

### Using Bullet for an unprepared order

Do not pay for urgency while the parcel is still waiting for packing or payment approval.

### Hiding the delivery deadline

“Fast delivery” is vague. Use “1–4 hours” or “within 24 hours” when the conditions apply.

### Measuring only average speed

A healthy average can hide severely late orders. Track on-time percentage and worst cases too.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Bullet always faster than Express?

Yes, based on the stated eligible service windows: Bullet is 1–4 hours and Express is within 24 hours. Actual performance also depends on parcel readiness, address and operating conditions.

### Is there a monthly subscription?

ZAJEX's FAQ says there is no monthly subscription and merchants pay for actual shipments. Check the merchant account or request a current quote for each service.

### Can both services be tracked?

Yes. ZAJEX provides shipment status from collection to delivery in its dashboard, while its API can return orders and their statuses.

### What about orders outside Riyadh?

ZAJEX says it can deliver to Saudi cities, typically within one to four business days depending on destination and operating conditions. The 1–4-hour Bullet service is for eligible Riyadh areas.

## The bottom line

Choosing between **ZAJEX Bullet and Express** is not about declaring one service better. It is about matching the order to the promised arrival time. Bullet is for a ready, urgent parcel in an eligible zone; Express is for a regular order where delivery within 24 hours is sufficient. Use both, show a clear promise and measure on-time performance, cost and customer satisfaction.
