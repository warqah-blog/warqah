Choosing a delivery provider does not end with label price or a headline speed promise. A shipment can be inexpensive yet need two attempts, arrive quickly with poor status updates, or appear successful in a carrier report while customer-service contacts and returns keep rising.

The more useful question is not simply **“Is ZAJEX good?”** It is: **Does ZAJEX produce the delivery result your store needs, on the services and routes you actually use, at a sustainable cost per completed order?**

<div class="answer-box">
<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Run a live pilot and separate Bullet, Express and intercity orders. Measure eight things: on-time delivery, first-attempt success, final delivery rate, failed delivery and return to origin, cost per successful order, tracking accuracy, exception-resolution time and COD settlement. If the complete outcome improves—not only the shipment price—the service is likely a good fit for your store.</p>
</div>

## Why delivery performance needs measurement

Delivery is both a customer experience and an operating cost. Every extra attempt consumes time. Every incomplete address raises failure risk. Every stale tracking event produces another “Where is my order?” contact.

Saudi Arabia's Transport General Authority reported more than 180 million parcels in 2024 and a sector-level 96% success rate within the agreed delivery duration. Those market figures are not a guarantee for any carrier or merchant account. They do show that **delivery within the agreed promise is a measurable outcome**, not merely a marketing phrase.

Your store still needs its own baseline. A Riyadh gift store serving urgent occasions does not share the same definition of success as a store sending non-urgent goods across several cities.

## Separate services and routes before calculating averages

[ZAJEX](https://zajex.co/en) presents two local options in Riyadh: **Bullet** for eligible orders within 1–4 hours and **Express** within 24 hours. Its Arabic FAQ also states that delivery outside Riyadh usually takes 1–4 business days depending on destination and operating conditions.

Do not place all orders in one average. Separate:

- Bullet orders in Riyadh.
- Express orders in Riyadh.
- Intercity shipping.
- Prepaid and cash-on-delivery orders.
- Standard and large or sensitive parcels.
- New and returning customers.

Mixing these segments can hide a strong service inside a weak average—or make a difficult service appear better because easy orders dominate the sample.

For the service decision itself, read [ZAJEX Bullet vs Express](/en/articles/zajex-bullet-vs-express-delivery/).

## 1. On-time delivery rate

This KPI asks how many orders arrived within the promise shown to the customer.

<div class="answer-box">
<p><strong>Formula:</strong> Orders delivered within the agreed window ÷ all delivered orders × 100.</p>
</div>

Measure Bullet against its 1–4-hour window and Express against 24 hours. Record when the parcel became ready and was handed over, as well as when delivery completed. Time spent in the merchant's own preparation process should not automatically be attributed to the delivery provider.

Break results down by area, weekday and handover hour. The data may reveal a cutoff-time problem rather than a service-wide failure.

## 2. First-attempt delivery success

An order may ultimately arrive after two calls and another attempt. A report showing only “delivered” hides this cost.

<div class="answer-box">
<p><strong>Formula:</strong> Orders completed on the first attempt ÷ orders sent out for delivery × 100.</p>
</div>

A stronger result normally signals better address data, customer coordination and operating cost. When it falls, separate no answer, inaccurate address, requested reschedule, refusal and unsuitable arrival time.

Do not treat “customer issue” as one universal reason. A precise reason shows whether the store should repair checkout data, pre-arrival messaging or COD confirmation.

## 3. Final delivery rate

This is the share of orders that eventually completed delivery out of all orders handed to the provider. It is broader than first-attempt success because an order delivered on a second attempt still counts as delivered.

<div class="answer-box">
<p><strong>Formula:</strong> Delivered orders ÷ orders handed to the provider × 100.</p>
</div>

Read the two KPIs together:

- High delivery and high first-attempt success: a healthy route.
- High delivery but low first-attempt success: good completion with extra cost and effort.
- Both low: investigate data, communication, coverage and service choice.

## 4. Failed delivery and return to origin

Failed delivery is not the same as a product return after receipt. The customer never completed receipt, and the parcel may enter another attempt or return to the merchant.

Calculate the rate, then classify causes consistently:

- Invalid phone number or no answer.
- Incomplete or out-of-coverage address.
- COD refusal.
- Unexpected total or payment method.
- Rescheduling request.
- Parcel or label issue.

An address problem is not solved by changing carriers alone. A COD refusal pattern begins before dispatch. Repeated failures in one area despite accurate data should be reviewed with ZAJEX as a coverage or operating issue.

For a complete distinction between failed delivery, returns and reverse logistics, see [Ecommerce returns in Saudi Arabia](/en/articles/ecommerce-returns-reverse-logistics-saudi-arabia/).

## 5. Cost per successfully delivered order

Label price is not true delivery cost. [ZAJEX's delivery-cost guide](https://zajex.co/ar/blog/ecommerce-delivery-cost-saudi-arabia) explains that attempts, returns and COD services may affect the final merchant cost under the applicable agreement.

For the same reporting period, combine:

- Base shipping charges.
- Additional-attempt fees where applicable.
- Return-to-origin or reverse-shipping cost.
- Customer-service time.
- COD processing and reconciliation effort.
- Delivery-related damage or repacking cost.

Then divide by successfully delivered orders—not generated labels.

<div class="answer-box">
<p><strong>Formula:</strong> Total delivery and failure cost during the period ÷ successfully delivered orders.</p>
</div>

A slightly higher shipment fee may be cheaper overall if it reduces repeated attempts, failure and support work.

## 6. Tracking accuracy and event timing

Tracking quality is more than having a working link. Status should match reality at a useful time.

Review a weekly sample:

- Does pickup appear after the parcel is actually handed over?
- Does “out for delivery” appear before courier arrival?
- Is a failure reason specific?
- Does delivery time match proof of delivery?
- Do events return to the store platform without manual entry or material delay?

ZAJEX presents tracking from pickup to arrival, but the merchant experience also depends on integration, data entry and internal procedures. Test one complete live order, not label creation alone.

## 7. Exception-resolution time

Not every order follows the happy path. The store may need to correct an address, adjust timing, understand a failed attempt or investigate a shipment whose status stopped moving.

Measure three times:

1. Time to the first useful response.
2. Time until an owner and next action are identified.
3. Time until the issue is actually closed.

A fast “we are checking” reply is not resolution. Record when the parcel returned to its route or a clear decision was made. Then identify the exception types that repeat most often.

## 8. COD collection and settlement accuracy

For cash on delivery, the journey does not end at the door. The shipment amount must match collection, the delivered order must appear in reports and settlement must follow the agreed cycle.

Track:

- COD delivery rate.
- Expected-versus-collected differences.
- Delivered shipments missing from settlement reports.
- Average days from delivery to settlement.
- Time required to close discrepancies.

Ask ZAJEX to document the settlement cycle, fees and reporting available to your merchant account because commercial terms can vary.

## How to run a fair ZAJEX pilot

### Define success before the pilot

Write down each service promise, coverage, measurement start time and definitions for failed delivery and another attempt. Do not change the definition after seeing the result.

### Use a representative sample

Do not test five orders from one district. Cover weekdays, important areas and cities, COD and prepaid orders, new and returning buyers, and normal product types.

### Keep merchant operations consistent

Validate addresses, prepare parcels on the same schedule and use clear labels and equivalent packaging. A comparison is not fair when data and fulfilment quality differ between samples.

### Compare with a baseline or alternative route

Use the store's previous figures or run a parallel pilot with comparable orders. Avoid relying on a generic benchmark that does not reflect your products or geography.

### Review the evidence with ZAJEX

Share cases and metrics rather than impressions. Ask why performance clusters by area or time, agree on an action, and repeat measurement in the next cycle.

## When might ZAJEX be a logical fit?

ZAJEX may fit merchants that need fast delivery for eligible Riyadh orders, clear integration and tracking, a choice between Bullet and Express, and intercity shipping under the available service.

Fit is not established by the service name. The result becomes convincing when:

- Most orders arrive inside the correct promise.
- First-attempt success remains stable.
- Ambiguous cases and manual contacts fall.
- Cost per successful delivery is reasonable after failure and returns.
- COD reports match shipments and amounts.
- The team can identify and resolve exceptions promptly.

## Bottom line

The best delivery provider is not necessarily the one with the lowest price or fastest written promise. It is the one that produces a repeatable result for your store's real orders.

Separate Bullet, Express and intercity shipping. Measure timing, first attempt, final delivery, failure, complete cost, tracking accuracy, exception handling and COD settlement. Use a fair sample, review the evidence with ZAJEX and repeat the pilot after improvements.

That turns delivery selection from an impression into a clear commercial decision: what worked, where it failed, what it cost and what must change before the store scales.
